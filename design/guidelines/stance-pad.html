1. A tap opens the pad, and it parks at the lower centre of the viewport, the same place every time — muscle memory is part of the control. A press and hold signs the modest positive outright, without opening anything.
  2. Three readouts, formatted alike: current opinion, your pick, resulting opinion — a label with the face and the numbers on the line below it, so the eye compares them without reading.
  3. The pick reads the edge being authored, not the bundle it joins — and it is the largest of the three. The anchor's words are not drawn; they stay in the accessibility tree.
  4. The current opinion sits above the field, the resulting one below. Two different numbers, never merged into one line.
  5. Nothing sits under the knob: a thumb covers exactly the spot where feedback would otherwise appear.
  6. The centre-lines are drawn dead ground — an inert axis is legible, not hidden.
  7. Releasing never commits. Set signs it; Cancel or an outside press stages nothing. One row: the walk-away left, the two decisions right.
  8. The axes are named on the field — Against / For, Less / More, the same four words the sliders use. A blank square says nothing about which direction means what.
  9. The circled ? sits in the corner, out of the readouts' reading order. It replaces the pad's body with four lines — what the field means, what commits, why the two faces can differ, what walking it back costs — and disables Set while it shows. A panel that grew below would push Set away from the thumb and defeat the parking.
