# The Corpus Linter — Concept

_Phase 1 of the standard engineering process: concept. Review closes this phase; the design candidate follows._

This document is the concept for `crates/cogra-linter`: one Rust binary that walks the corpus — Markdown prose and compiled-platform source — and mechanically discharges the checkable obligations of the four adopted discipline documents, the label calculus, the kind registry, the identity adjudication procedure, and the interchange conventions. It states why the linter must exist, who consumes its findings, what it must do — every requirement traced to the discipline clause, architecture decision, or adoption datum that demands it — what it will not do, how it will be verified, and what the phases ahead look like. It decides nothing the architecture has already decided: that document is the ratified design, this one sits below it and cites it. The concept review's rulings are recorded in place.

The document practices the labeling discipline: the label at each heading or environment head is that environment's mint; a parenthesized label in running text is a same-owner citation; material in fenced blocks and double-backtick spans is displayed without participating, which is where every token this document merely quotes sits. The document title mints nothing. Every label minted here has area `lint`. Under the owner partition this file belongs to `pkg.cogra-linter`, a different owner from the architecture document, whose area is `linter`: two owners may mint one label text without collision, and a distinct area keeps the two documents' labels legible apart, as the interchange crate's artifacts keep theirs apart from the conventions they implement. Imported citations use the prefixes registered in `corpus-adoption.toml`: `LBL` for the label calculus, `KND` for the kind registry, `IDN` for the identity adjudication procedure, `ICX` for the interchange conventions, and `ARCH` for the linter architecture.

## Purpose · `sec:lint:purpose`

**Formulation (Purpose)** · `formul:lint:purpose`

Four disciplines are adopted and none is enforced. Each was written to end in a gate — (`[LBL-gate:labels:implementation]`), (`[KND-gate:kinds:adoption]`), (`[IDN-gate:identity:implementation]`) — whose clauses are mechanical, and each closes that gate with the same sentence: it is dischargeable from that document and the corpus's adoption data alone, no check consulting another document. A checker is therefore not one possible consumer of these documents but the discharge mechanism they were written for, and the calculus says as much of itself — the checker and the register generator are its only consumers (`[LBL-cav:labels:non-normativity]`). An adopted-but-unchecked discipline is worse than an unadopted one: citations dangle silently, registers go stale while still looking authoritative, and a corpus that cannot say what it means by a name has paid the discipline's cost without buying its benefit. The cost is already sunk and the drift already measurable — twenty-nine committed Markdown files carry label-shaped prose spans, ~119 occurrences among them are imported citations across five owners, and the docs trees carry dozens of same-owner citations that resolve nowhere (`rep:lint:first-corpus`). Two ruled inventory profiles cover 284 test functions and ~42 module definitions whose labels are derived, not authored, and which no hand can maintain or verify at that scale. The linter is what turns four essays into four enforced disciplines.

**Signature (Consumers)** · `sig:lint:consumers`

Four consumers, present and planned. The corpus authors, who receive located diagnostics as they write and whose renames the linter makes safe by breaking exactly what the rename broke (`[LBL-metathm:labels:warrant-lapse]`). Continuous integration, which runs the corpus-wide check as the calculus's gate requires and fails the build on findings, distinguishing findings from crashes by exit code (`[ARCH-req:linter:diagnostics-not-panics]`). The gates themselves, whose clauses the linter is the instrument of: the architecture's own gate closes on a citation check the finished linter runs over the document that specified it (`[ARCH-gate:linter:architecture-review]`), and the identity gate requires the corpus's full checks to pass leaving no uncommitted generated changes (`[IDN-gate:identity:implementation]`). And the generated registers the disciplines call for — the per-owner label registers of the test profile, the kind registry's headline counts, the acceptee's companion attestation register — which have a generator only when the linter has one (`[ARCH-rule:linter:register-freshness]`). No consumer outside this repository exists in v1; the disciplines are corpus-generic and the crate layout anticipates extraction, but a second corpus is not a v1 consumer (`[ARCH-dec:linter:crate-layout]`).

**Requirement (Nothing about the corpus in the binary)** · `req:lint:adoption-data-only`

The linter is generic over corpora and the configuration is this corpus's adoption. All seven parametric data of the calculus, the kind registry's own adoption data, and this corpus's additions — the carrier's exclusions, the banned-token sets — live in one committed file, and changing any of them is a reviewed edit to that file rather than a code change (`[ARCH-sig:linter:adoption-data]`). The consequences are concrete and load-bearing: path rules are literal prefixes with no pattern dialect, honoring the no-regex decision even in configuration (`[ARCH-dec:linter:no-regex]`); the kind registry's classification tables are parsed from the registry document rather than transcribed (`[ARCH-dec:linter:registry-as-data]`); and a new package needs no code change and no decision, joining the prefix family by its derivation rule (`[LBL-sig:labels:owners]`).

**Requirement (The linter is the register generator)** · `req:lint:register-generator`

Every generated register the disciplines call for has one generator, and it is the linter's regeneration mode: the per-owner label registers of the inventory profiles, the kind registry's headline counts, and the acceptee's companion attestation register alike (`[KND-req:kinds:attestation-register]`), (`[ARCH-rule:linter:register-freshness]`). The corpus's adoption data names the acceptee who owns the registry's extension set, evidence base, statuses, generator, and register, and adopts the registry's own defaults for them: the extension set is empty and the evidence base is the registry edition's in full (`[KND-sig:kinds:acceptee]`). One consequence of a generator that postdates its registers is stated rather than discovered: a register that has never been generated has no committed bytes to compare against, so the check reports it as staged rather than stale, and the first generation run commits the initial registers and arms exact byte comparison from then on. Staged is a state of the corpus, not a weakening of the rule — every register that exists is compared exactly, and the linter still writes nothing in check mode.

**Requirement (Determinism and order independence)** · `req:lint:determinism`

Two runs over one tree emit one output: diagnostics sort by path, then byte offset, then rule identifier, and no verdict depends on traversal order (`[ARCH-req:linter:determinism]`). Determinism is not a nicety here but the precondition of the central mechanism — a register compared byte-exactly against its committed form is only meaningful if regeneration is deterministic (`[ARCH-rule:linter:register-freshness]`), and order independence is what the calculus proves of itself (`[LBL-metathm:labels:order-independence]`).

**Requirement (Failures are diagnostics)** · `req:lint:diagnostics`

Malformed input never panics the linter, and every failure is scoped exactly as the discipline scopes it: an unpaired backtick fails its block while the rest of the file resolves, an opening acute unclosed at a region's end fails there, an unreadable tree is a reported traversal failure, and a frontend parse error is a hard located diagnostic rather than a silently skipped region (`[ARCH-req:linter:diagnostics-not-panics]`), (`[LBL-judg:labels:participation]`), (`[LBL-cav:labels:coexistence]`). Scoped register generation ignores unrelated owners' defects while the corpus-wide check still validates everything.

**Requirement (Timing)** · `req:lint:timing`

Every run reports wall time per phase — pre-tokenize, harvest, resolve, judge, render — and the first measured full-corpus run sets the budget recorded beside the CI lane that invokes the linter; exceeding it thereafter is a finding, not a cost to absorb (`[ARCH-req:linter:timing]`).

**Requirement (Workspace discipline)** · `req:lint:workspace-discipline`

The crate obeys every standing repository rule — `cargo fmt`, `clippy -D warnings`, no `unwrap` in library code, `thiserror` and `anyhow` for the error surface, no SQL — and the three fixed constraints of the architecture, which are the decisions most easily eroded in implementation and are therefore restated as requirements rather than left as context: every in-memory graph is a petgraph type used directly, with filtered views where a restricted graph is wanted and free functions where a domain operation is (`[ARCH-dec:linter:petgraph-first-class]`), (`[ARCH-rem:linter:views-not-wrappers]`); no regular-expression engine appears on the analysis path, the one signed exception living inside the interchange crate and nowhere else (`[ARCH-dec:linter:no-regex]`), (`[ARCH-dec:linter:cddl-regexp-library]`); and analysis runs over syntax trees, raw bytes being consulted only where the AST hands back a span whose delimiter form the discipline needs, and in the pre-tokenizer pass (`[ARCH-dec:linter:ast-frontends]`). Dependencies are the architecture's (`[ARCH-tab:linter:dependencies]`), each verified against its own current documentation before the first line depends on it.

## Scope boundary · `sec:lint:scope`

**Convention (Out of scope)** · `conv:lint:out-of-scope`

Version 1 is a checker: it reports, and humans and later tooling repair (`[ARCH-formul:linter:charter]`). The single exception is not repair but regeneration — an explicit mode that rewrites a generated register in place, never invoked by the check (`[ARCH-rule:linter:register-freshness]`) — and it invents nothing: an authored label no author has chosen is an absence rather than an opening, met as a defect to surface (`[LBL-inv:labels:generated-compliance]`). The corpus's pending migrations are consequently not the linter's work: the sweep of ~1210 plain line comments, the ~42 module definitions owed an inner documentation comment, and the reforming of the docs trees' upstream references into displayed spans (`rep:lint:first-corpus`) are separate tasks, and the linter's contribution to each is the located diagnostic.

Frontends are sliced, and a language with no frontend has no scanned regions: Markdown and Rust are the first slice, the web frontend the second, Kotlin the third behind the precondition that a first-party grammar parses the whole Android corpus to zero error nodes, and LaTeX remains deferred (`[ARCH-conv:linter:web-frontend]`), (`[ARCH-dec:linter:kotlin-tree-sitter]`), (`[ARCH-open:linter:latex-frontend]`). SQL, GraphQL, TOML, Kotlin build scripts, shell, JSON, YAML and SVG get no frontend at all in v1; their files stay in the carrier and stay owned, carry no occurrences, and are vacuously in good standing (`[LBL-judg:labels:minting]`). The profile signature is likewise small on purpose: two Rust profiles, and no Markdown, TypeScript or Kotlin profile, because a profile whose frontend does not exist cannot compute a census and a half-computed census breaks the two-pass staging outright (`[LBL-inv:labels:two-pass]`). The two Rust profiles are themselves staged: inventory admits nothing partial, so a profile enters the adoption data together with the migration that makes it satisfiable and not before — the test profile when the first register generation lands (`req:lint:register-generator`), the module profile when the ~42 inner documentation comments do. Until then each is registered and outside the effective profile signature, and its kind stays reserved-but-ungoverned, where a bare occurrence is a hard failure awaiting its derivation (`[LBL-sig:labels:reserved-kinds]`). The designated typed-data classes and the citation-index designations are both empty and staged, so their checks have empty domains rather than absent implementations.

Three boundaries are not slicing but principle. The linter mints no digest and adds none, its freshness comparison being exact bytes and its no-identity outcome the recorded stop (`[IDN-case:identity:no-identity]`). Nothing outside the carrier is examined: the Layer 1 interface document sits outside the label graph entirely — no owner, no prefix, no scanned region — so nothing in it resolves and nothing in it can fail, until the upstream repository becomes public and joins as a citable owner. And the graph the linter builds is documentation, never semantic input: no compiler, build system, packager, or release tool consumes it, and no version of the software this corpus documents moves because a label was renamed (`[LBL-cav:labels:non-normativity]`).

**Convention (Clauses the linter does not discharge)** · `conv:lint:non-mechanical`

The gates are the acceptance criteria, but not every clause in them is a check, and the ones that are not belong to named authorities rather than to the binary. From the kind registry, the linter checks token distinctness, hybrid composition, catalogued pairs, and register currency; it does not judge that a token is one per *concept* or that a synonym class is convergent — those are the registry authority's editorial rulings — nor does it adjudicate attestation evidence, which the acceptee owns first-hand (`[KND-sig:kinds:acceptee]`), (`[KND-cav:kinds:attestation-limits]`). From the identity discipline the linter takes exactly one standing duty and one prohibition, as its charter says: exact byte comparison of committed generated publications, and no digest without a walked adjudication (`[ARCH-formul:linter:charter]`). The remainder of that gate — classifying every existing digest, recording admissions and stops, demonstrating that a recipe delivers its class's properties, binding evidence roles and release manifests — is a human review duty over the corpus, and the linter neither performs nor certifies it; the acyclicity check over an identity graph enters only when identity checking itself lands (`[ARCH-tab:linter:judgments-as-queries]`). From the label calculus, the final gate clause — that the corpus-wide check passes in continuous integration — is discharged by the CI lane, not by the binary that lane runs.

## Verification · `sec:lint:verification`

**Convention (Gates as acceptance criteria)** · `conv:lint:gates-as-acceptance`

The linter is accepted against the disciplines' own gates, clause by clause, and against nothing else: (`[LBL-gate:labels:implementation]`) in full, (`[KND-gate:kinds:adoption]`) and (`[IDN-gate:identity:implementation]`) in the parts (`conv:lint:non-mechanical`) leaves to it, and (`[ARCH-gate:linter:architecture-review]`) whose closing clause is a check the finished linter runs over the document that specified it. Each gate states that it is dischargeable from its own document and the corpus's adoption data alone, which fixes the shape of the acceptance suite: a clause is discharged by a test naming the clause, over this corpus's ruled adoption data, and no test consults a third document. The interchange conventions carry no gate of their own, and need none here: their obligations are discharged by the sibling crate, whose audit and commissioning phases have closed, and the linter's whole duty toward them is delegation — R19 of (`tab:lint:functional`).

**Convention (Metatheorems as test obligations)** · `conv:lint:metatheorems-as-tests`

The label calculus proves four metatheorems about the system the linter implements, and each becomes an executable obligation rather than a paragraph. Order independence becomes a property test that shuffled traversal orders over one tree emit byte-identical output (`[LBL-metathm:labels:order-independence]`). No self-support becomes a test that an index row never sustains its own membership, and that removing a document's last body citation of a label stales the committed index (`[LBL-metathm:labels:no-self-support]`). Warrant lapse becomes a property test over transitions of a covered asset: renaming its identifier, changing its classification, moving it across packages, or removing it from the census dangles exactly the citations of the facet or boundary that moved, while moving it within its package lapses nothing (`[LBL-metathm:labels:warrant-lapse]`). Presentation invariance becomes a test that a migration re-forming occurrences while preserving every label value leaves every registry, every harvested set, and every hash computed from them unchanged (`[LBL-metathm:labels:presentation-invariance]`). The kind registry proves no metatheorems and instead asserts invariants over its own tables, which makes it its own first test corpus: it classifies its own heads, so parsing it and validating it are one exercise (`[ARCH-dec:linter:registry-as-data]`). The identity discipline prescribes no construction and its obligation is a policy assertion — that the crate contains no digest. The interchange metatheorems were already discharged as executable obligations inside the interchange crate and are not re-tested here. Property-test and fuzz sizing, and the budgets of (`req:lint:timing`), are the design candidate's to fix.

**Report (The first corpus)** · `rep:lint:first-corpus`

The four discipline documents are the truest acceptance test, because they practice the disciplines they define: each declares its own labeling convention in its preamble, mints one label per environment head, carries no environment numbering, and classifies its own heads by the registry. *The linter lints its own four discipline documents* is therefore the first honest milestone, followed by the architecture document and the interchange crate's phase artifacts — the material already written under the discipline.

The wider corpus is further from clean than the adoption data records, and the concept states the gap rather than discovering it in implementation. Measured 2026-08-25 by raw-text sweep, which can only over-count, participation being an AST fact this sweep cannot see: twenty-nine committed Markdown files outside the vendored Layer 1 document carry label-shaped single-backtick prose spans, where the adoption data records seven label-bearing files; imported citations numbered ~119 across five prefixes when this document joined them, against the 113 recorded five days earlier, the difference being the interchange audit and commissioning notes written since — a hand-kept measurement decaying inside a week, which is the purpose argument in miniature. In the three docs trees whose owners the adoption data records as carrying no label occurrences, 37 parenthesized label-shaped spans and roughly eight bare ones occur; their targets are names of the Layer 1 interface document, which is outside the carrier, so each parenthesized span is a same-owner citation resolving nowhere and each bare span would be read as a mint. Rust sources carry 88 label-shaped backtick spans inside documentation comments, where the code syntax is the acute — near-miss warnings rather than occurrences, and precisely the class the calculus asks the checker to warn on. Two bare label-shaped spans occur in the adoption notes themselves, a file whose preamble declares that its label-shaped references are written in double backticks throughout, and one of the two is the very span the surrounding sentence describes as a hazard. Two further spans, imported-citation-shaped and sitting inside fenced illustrations of the calculus, participate in nothing yet would be counted by any text sweep — the cleanest small demonstration of why the analysis is AST-based (`[ARCH-ansatz:linter:regex-scanning]`). Those docs-tree spans are references to the upstream Layer 1 owner, and they are reformed into displayed double-backtick spans before the linter enforces anything — carrying their meaning as references, and promoted to imported citations on the day that owner becomes citable. The sweep is a queued migration, verified by the linter once it exists.

Acceptance is scoped accordingly, because a red gate teaches a team to ignore the gate. Version 1 is accepted on a clean run over the material written under the discipline — the four discipline documents, the architecture, and the interchange and linter phase artifacts — and the linter enters CI as a failing gate over exactly that set and an advisory one over the rest. The failing set grows as each migration lands, and when the last one has, the advisory half is empty and the gate enforces corpus-wide. That end state is what (`req:lint:timing`) budgets and what the commissioning note records.

## Phases · `sec:lint:phases`

**Roadmap (Phase plan)** · `preview:lint:phase-plan`

The standard engineering process, instantiated for this crate — each phase closes on an explicit artifact, and the next does not start until the previous closes:

| Phase | Artifact that closes it | Closed by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Concept | this document | review ruling |
| Design candidate | design.md: module map, the node and edge weight enums, error taxonomy, the first slice's public shape, dependency justifications, test plan sized | review ruling |
| Review | rulings recorded into the artifacts; open questions emptied | the human |
| Implementation | the slices in sequence, tests green, `make ci` green | gate of the design |
| Audit | adversarial review + property and fuzz results, findings dispositioned | audit report |
| Commissioning | the corpus-wide check runs in CI; runtime budget measured and recorded | commissioning note |

The design candidate can lean on the architecture rather than re-deriving it, which is what makes this concept lean: the pipeline, the corpus-graph model, the frontend conventions, and the fixed constraints are ratified there. What still wants a design pass before code is the module map against the drawn boundaries (`[ARCH-dec:linter:crate-layout]`), the two weight enums whose variants the judgments query (`[ARCH-tab:linter:node-edge-vocabulary]`), and the public shape of the first slice. The audit phase is planned, not optional: on the previous run of this process the deferred hazards a design named came due there exactly as named.

## Concept gate · `sec:lint:gate`

**Gate (Concept review)** · `gate:lint:concept-review`

The design-candidate phase is blocked until all of the following hold:

- the purpose and the consumer set (`formul:lint:purpose`), (`sig:lint:consumers`) are confirmed;
- the requirements (`tab:lint:functional`), (`req:lint:adoption-data-only`), (`req:lint:register-generator`), (`req:lint:determinism`), (`req:lint:diagnostics`), (`req:lint:timing`), (`req:lint:workspace-discipline`) are confirmed complete against the four disciplines and the ruled adoption data, or amended;
- the scope boundary (`conv:lint:out-of-scope`) and the division of the gates' clauses (`conv:lint:non-mechanical`) are confirmed;
- the verification frame (`conv:lint:gates-as-acceptance`), (`conv:lint:metatheorems-as-tests`) is confirmed as the test plan's frame, and the scoped acceptance of (`rep:lint:first-corpus`) is confirmed as the target v1 is measured against.
